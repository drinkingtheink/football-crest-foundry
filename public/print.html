How to Print Your Crest

Design it here, export it, print it — no design-shop round-trip.

Every crest you export from Crest Foundry is print- and merch-ready. The files come out with a transparent background and no font dependencies, so they drop straight into a sticker press, embroidery digitizer, or apparel printer. Here's how to go from your design to a physical crest.

1. Pick your format

⬇ SVG — for anything that scales

A true vector file with all text converted to outlines, so it needs no fonts and stays razor-sharp at any size. This is what a print shop, vinyl cutter, or embroidery digitizer wants. Use it for die-cut / vinyl stickers, large apparel prints, banners, signage, and patches.

⬇ PNG — for quick raster runs

A transparent, high-resolution image (~1600×1920 pixels — roughly a 5×6″ print at 300 DPI). Perfect for die-cut stickers, patches, and small apparel prints, or any service that only accepts raster uploads.

⧉ Copy — for a fast paste

Copies the same clean, transparent PNG straight to your clipboard, ready to paste into a chat, a doc, or a merch service's upload form.

2. Why your files are already print-ready

  • Transparent background — the crest sits on shirts, mugs, and sticker stock with no white box around it.
  • Outlined text (SVG) — glyphs are real vector shapes, so there are no missing-font surprises when a shop opens your file.
  • True vector (SVG) — scales from a lapel pin to a banner without going fuzzy.

3. Send it to a print or merch service

Plenty of options exist depending on what you want to make:

  • Custom sticker printers — upload the SVG (for die-cut / vinyl) or the PNG (for standard stickers).
  • Print-on-demand apparel & merch — for shirts, hoodies, mugs, tote bags, and more; upload the PNG, or the SVG if they accept vector.
  • Local vinyl, sign, or embroidery shops — hand them the SVG; it's the format their equipment expects.

The basic flow is the same everywhere: design your crest → ⬇ export (or ⧉ copy) → upload it to the service → choose your size and material.

4. A few gotchas

  • Die-cut & vinyl need vector. If a shop offers a custom cut line around your crest, send the SVG.
  • Embroidery and patches like simplicity. Fewer colors and bolder shapes reproduce best in thread; very fine detail may be dropped when digitized.
  • Check the shop's minimum resolution if you're printing large from the PNG. For anything bigger than a few inches, prefer the SVG so it stays crisp.
  • Mind the licenses. Some icons require attribution and some uses may involve trademarks — see the Terms of Use and the app's About panel.
